04 / A LETTER FROM GIGI

For your 40th birthday

To My Ama,

Happy 40th birthday, Ama!

I wanted to make something special for your birthday—not just a simple greeting, not just a picture with “Happy Birthday,” and definitely not just a cake. I wanted to make something that could hold all the things I don't always know how to say to you in person.

So I decided to make you a little corner of the internet just for you. 😭 HAHAHAHA.

But behind all the jokes and the website and everything else, there's actually a lot I want to tell you.

First of all, thank you.

Thank you for being my dad. Thank you for everything you've done for me and for our family, whether they're big things or little things that I sometimes don't even notice.

Thank you for working hard for our family. Thank you for providing for us, for worrying about us, for protecting us, and for making sure we're okay.

Thank you for the sacrifices you've made that I may not fully understand yet because I'm still growing up.

Thank you for every time you've put our family before yourself.

Thank you for every time you've been tired but still continued doing what you needed to do.

Thank you for every time you tried to teach me something, even when I didn't want to listen.

Thank you for correcting me when I was wrong, even when I didn't appreciate it at the time.

And thank you for loving me even during the moments when I probably wasn't showing you much love back.

I'm sorry, Ama.

I'm sorry for all the lies I've told you in the past. I'm sorry for the times I wasn't completely honest with you, the things I've hidden, and the excuses I've made.

I'm sorry for the times I've said something that wasn't true because I was scared of what would happen if I told you the truth.

I'm sorry for the times I made you question whether you could trust me.

I know that trust isn't something I can ask for just because I'm saying sorry. I know I have to earn it back through my actions.

I know that some mistakes don't disappear just because we apologize for them.

But I genuinely regret those moments.

If I could go back and handle those situations differently, I would. I would tell the truth sooner. I would communicate better. I would think before making decisions.

I can't change what happened before, but I can change what I do from now on. And that's something I want to work on.

I'm sorry for my shortcomings, too.

Being your eldest daughter sometimes makes me feel like I'm supposed to have everything figured out.

Like I'm supposed to know how to make the right decisions. Like I'm supposed to be responsible. Like I'm supposed to set an example. Like I'm supposed to know how to handle everything.

But the truth is... I'm still learning too.

I'm sorry for the times I've been irresponsible. I'm sorry for the times I've been stubborn, talked back, or let my emotions control the way I acted.

I'm sorry for the times I've complained about things without realizing how much you were already doing for us.

I'm sorry for the times I expected things from you without thinking about your side first.

I'm sorry for the times I didn't help when I could have, for the times I needed to be reminded again and again, and for the times I didn't listen to your advice.

I'm sorry for the times my choices disappointed you or made you worry.

I'm sorry for the moments when I could have been more patient, more understanding, and more responsible.

And I'm sorry for the times I forgot that you are also just a person trying your best.

You're my dad, but you're also someone who gets tired. Someone who gets stressed. Someone who has responsibilities. Someone who has problems. Someone who also needs rest.

Sometimes I get so caught up in being your daughter that I forget to look at you as a person who is also doing his best. I'm sorry for that.

I know I still have a lot to learn.

I don't want to pretend that I've suddenly become perfect just because I'm writing you a birthday letter. I haven't.

I'm still going to make mistakes. I'm still going to have moments where I get emotional. I'm still going to have things I don't understand.

But I don't want to use “I'm still young” as an excuse to never improve.

I want to learn from my mistakes. I want to become more responsible. I want to become more honest. I want to communicate instead of hiding things.

I want to learn how to control my emotions better. I want to become someone you can trust.

And I want to become someone my younger siblings can look up to— not because I'm perfect, but because they can see me learning from my mistakes and becoming better.

Thank you for being patient with me.

I don't know how many times you've had to tell me the same thing. I don't know how many times you've had to remind me. I don't know how many times you've worried about me.

But somehow, you're still here. Still my dad. Still teaching me. Still correcting me. Still caring about me.

There are probably things I won't understand until I'm older. There are sacrifices you'll never even tell me about. There are things you've done for me that I'll only realize years from now.

And when that happens, I hope I'll remember to look back and think: “My dad really did so much for me.”

What I want you to know.

I don't want you to think that the mistakes I've made mean I don't appreciate you. They don't.

I don't want you to think that every argument means I don't love you. It doesn't.

I don't want you to think that every time I disagree with you, I don't respect you.

Sometimes I just don't know how to express myself properly yet. I'm still figuring myself out.

I'm still learning what I believe in, what I want, what I care about, and what kind of person I want to become.

But through all of that change, one thing isn't changing: You're still my Ama.

Your 40th birthday.

You're officially 40 now. 😭 FOUR. ZERO. HAHAHAHAHA.

But seriously, I hope this new chapter of your life is a good one.

I hope you have more reasons to smile. More peaceful days. More time to rest. More chances to enjoy the things you love. And more memories with our family.

Today isn't just about celebrating the fact that you're turning 40. It's about celebrating you.

The dad who raised me. The dad who continues to teach me. The dad who has seen me at my best and at my worst. The dad who has watched me grow up. The dad who has had to deal with my chaos. 😭

And somehow... you survived. HAHAHAHAHA.

One promise.

I can't promise that I'll never make another mistake. I can't promise that I'll never disappoint you. I can't promise that I'll suddenly become the perfect daughter.

But I can promise that I'll keep trying.

I'll try to be more honest. I'll try to listen more. I'll try to understand before reacting. I'll try to be more responsible.

I'll try to appreciate the things you do instead of taking them for granted.

I'll try to become someone you can trust. And whenever I mess up, I hope I'll have the courage to admit it, apologize, and learn from it.

Because I don't want to stay the same person who made those mistakes. I want to grow.

Thank you for everything.

I don't think one birthday website could ever be enough to repay everything you've done for me.

But I hope this reminds you that your daughter sees you. Maybe not always. Maybe not perfectly. But I do.

I see your sacrifices. I see your effort. I see the things you do for our family.

And even if I don't always know how to say it... I'm grateful.

I'm grateful that you're my dad. I'm grateful for the memories we've made. I'm grateful for the lessons you've taught me. I'm grateful for the chances you've given me to learn from my mistakes.

And I'm grateful that I get to celebrate your 40th birthday with you.

So, Ama... thank you. I'm sorry. I love you.

I hope you have the happiest 40th birthday. You deserve a day where you can just relax, smile, eat good food, and enjoy being celebrated.

Happy 40th Birthday, Ama. 🎂🌲💚

I love you more than I probably say.

And no matter how much I grow up, I'll always be your daughter.

Love,
Gigi 💚
